\subsection{Abstract formulation of the multi-phase optimal control problem}
\label{sub:abstract_formulation}

The vehicle is a rigid body, not a point mass (\cref{apx:eom}). Its state
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model_state}
x=\bigl(r,\theta,\phi,\,u,v,w,\,q_0,q_1,q_2,q_3,\,\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3\bigr)\in\mathcal X\subseteq\mathbb R^{13},
\end{equation}
augmented by mass, recession depth $s$ and modal state $(\eta,\dot\eta)$
where the corresponding mode or coupling is active, carries geocentric
position, body-frame velocity, attitude quaternion and body rates as
components.
The attitude coordinates are redundant: every admissible state satisfies
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model_quaternion_norm}
q_0^{2}+q_1^{2}+q_2^{2}+q_3^{2}=1,
\end{equation}
so the thirteen coordinates carry twelve degrees of freedom.
The control
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:model_control}
c=\bigl(\vec\delta,\,\vec\tau_{\mathrm{rcs}},\,\varsigma\bigr)\in U,
\end{equation}
collects flap deflections $\vec\delta$, reaction-control moments
$\vec\tau_{\mathrm{rcs}}$ and, in a propulsive mode, throttle $\varsigma$;
$U\subset\mathbb R^{n_c}$ is compact. \todo{list deflection, RCS, and throttle limits (one citation each). compactness follows.}

Crucially the incidence angles
$(\alpha,\beta)$ are \emph{outputs} of the attitude solution
(\cref{eq:apx_incidence}), not commanded quantities: trim is not assumed
solvable\todo{elaborate}, and attitudes the vehicle cannot hold are excluded automatically
rather than by fiat. The reachable set cannot contain a state reached only by an
unattainable incidence.
