\title{\textbf{Certified analytical bounds for hypersonic reachable sets}\\[0.4em]
  {\large\normalfont\textit{A measure-theoretic profile decomposition in exact
  interval arithmetic}}}
\author{\textbf{Ethan Knox}}
\affil{\small\textit{Independent Researcher, Champaign, IL}}
\date{Preliminary PhD Research Proposal \\[0.5em] \today}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The reachable set of a nonlinear control system can be embedded in a space
of occupation measures, where the dynamics become a linear constraint and
the reachable set is read off the support of a terminal measure. This
research asks what that embedding yields under two departures from the
standard program: when the vector field is not a function but a set-valued
map, enclosed in exact interval arithmetic; and when the measure family
loses tightness in a singular limit. Rescaled by the fast timescale, mass
concentrates on a low-dimensional slow manifold, and the limit admits a
profile decomposition in the manner of Lions' concentration
alternative---a finite-dimensional skeleton, one boundary-layer bubble per
event, and a compact residual of order~$\sqrt{\eps}$. Almost-orthogonality
of the scaling groups, transported from the Bahouri--G\'erard construction
to defect measures under bounded adversarial uncertainty, is what makes the
decomposition a bound rather than an ansatz. The constants that size it are
required in closed form, derived from the physical constraint set and the
aerodynamic interval widths rather than from a numerical solver.
Hypersonic entry supplies the instance: a six-degree-of-freedom vehicle
whose thin-atmosphere limit furnishes the singular perturbation, whose
equilibrium-glide condition furnishes the slow manifold, and whose skip
events furnish the bubbles. The result is a certificate rather than a
computation: explicit algebraic bounds, valid uniformly over the entire
admissible family, in the minimal physical state space.
\end{abstract}

\section{The analytical problem}
%==========================================================================
The object to be bounded is the reachable set of an atmospheric entry
vehicle---every state it can be driven to under admissible control---which
is what safety and threat analyses need enclosed. The flight-mechanics
specifics are deferred: \cref{sec:application} states why entry is the
right instance and what its state space looks like%
\ifsummary
, and the coupled dynamics---frames, state vector, translational and
rotational equations, the aerodynamic bridge from attitude to force, and
the slow-manifold condition---are carried in a companion appendix omitted
from this copy%
\else
, and \cref{apx:dyn} carries the coupled dynamics in full%
\fi
. What matters first is that the
problem is an analytical one before it is an aerospace one, and that it
fails for two independent reasons---the dimension of the state, and the
provenance of the guarantee.

\subsection{Reachable sets as occupation measures}\label{sec:embedding}
Embed the hybrid flight dynamics into a Banach space of occupation
measures. The nonlinear flight mechanics $\dot x = f_q(x,c,t)$ become
\emph{linear}: the Liouville equation is a linear constraint on a measure
$\mu$ over $[0,T]\times\calX\times\calU$, and
$\operatorname{supp}\mu_T$ outer-approximates the reachable set. So far
this is the occupation-measure program of Lasserre, Henrion and
collaborators~\cite{lasserre2008nonlinear,vinter1993convex}. The embedding buys linearity
at the cost of uniqueness, and the way it is recovered is standard: a
trajectory family satisfying the Liouville constraint lies in the convex
hull of the generalized curves, so measures correspond to convex mixtures
of trajectories rather than to one of them~\cite{henrion2014convex,ball1989version}.
The same relaxation is what admits the interval envelope, and that is the
first thing that has to be built rather than borrowed. Because the true
aerodynamics are transcendental, they are enclosed in strict intervals, and
a vector field selected from an interval is a set-valued map; the
resulting differential inclusion is the object the measure must describe,
not the nominal field~\cite{filippov1988differential,aubin1984differential}. Occupation-measure
theory for inclusions of exactly this kind already exists and must be cited
as prior art: it defines an occupational invariant measure without
assuming solution uniqueness, which is the same move made
here~\cite{artstein1999invariant}. What it assumes is worth stating plainly,
because it is where the entry problem is likely to fail: the vector fields
must satisfy a linear growth condition, and the singular-perturbation
results additionally require a uniform bound on the fast state along the
whole $\eps$-family~\cite{artstein1999invariant,artstein2000value}.

\begin{openitem}{Liouville equation for differential inclusions}
The current framework embeds the flight mechanics
$\dot x = f_q(x,c,t)$ as a linear Liouville constraint on an occupation
measure $\mu$ over $[0,T]\times\calX\times\calU$. By bounding true
non-polynomial aerodynamics within strict intervals, the vector field
becomes a differential inclusion $\dot x \in \calF(x,c,t)$. We must
formulate a robust Liouville equation capable of acting on set-valued
maps, and rigorously prove that the support of the resulting occupation
measure, $\operatorname{supp}\mu_T$, securely outer-approximates all
possible trajectories generated by any vector field selected from the
interval inclusion.
\end{openitem}

\subsection{Why the guarantee must be exact}\label{sec:provenance}
The standard polynomial barrier with coefficients obtained from an
interior-point solver has analytical \emph{form} but inherits the solver's
accuracy~\cite{prajna2007framework}, so it is not a certificate; a numerical
solver's failure modes are documented, and they include the loss of strict
feasibility on which every rounding-based scheme
depends~\cite{roux2018validating,peyrl2008computing}. Provenance is not a
matter of care. Interval arithmetic is the setting in which a guarantee
has meant, for forty years, an enclosure rather than an approximation, and
the property that makes an enclosure valid at all---inclusion
isotonicity---is exactly what a reimplementation loses first; not every law
of real arithmetic survives the move to intervals, which is the original
form of the wrapping effect~\cite{moore2009introduction,nedialkov1999validated}. Applied
interval analysis supplies the machinery the pipeline would be built from,
and it is deliberately conservative: certified set inversion, deterministic
intersection geometries, and a customized exact-arithmetic integrator to
ensure floating-point inaccuracies do not compromise the final
bounds~\cite{jaulin2001applied}.

\begin{openitem}{Exact-arithmetic computational pipeline}
The proposal demands a guarantee verified in exact arithmetic rather than
produced by a numerical solver. Floating-point inaccuracies inherent to
standard simulation tools compromise the analytical certificate. Must
implement a custom, exact-arithmetic interval integrator (likely leveraging
C/C++ or specialized rational arithmetic libraries). This pipeline must
process the hybrid dynamics, manage the boundary-layer bubble transitions,
and compute the intersection geometries deterministically to verify the
final bounds.
\end{openitem}

\section{The singular limit and its profile decomposition}
\label{sec:profile}
%==========================================================================
This section carries the new content. The instance supplies a small
parameter---in the entry problem the thin-atmosphere ratio
$\eps_{\mathrm{atm}} = H_s/R_e \to 0$, where $H_s$ is the atmospheric
scale height and $R_e$ the planetary radius---and the question is what the
occupation-measure family does in that limit.

\subsection{Loss of tightness and the defect measure}
Rescaled by the phugoid-to-secular
time ratio (which, as in the classical Chapman--Loh variables, enters as
$\sqrt{\eps_{\mathrm{atm}}}$, not
$\eps_{\mathrm{atm}}$~\cite{chapman1959approximate,loh1968reentry}), the occupation-measure
family \emph{is not tight}. Mass concentrates onto the low-dimensional
equilibrium-glide slow manifold $\Lambda = 1$. In the 3-DOF (point-mass)
approximation this is the scalar condition
$L\cos\sigma/[m(g - V^2/r)] = 1$; in the full 6-DOF formulation the lift
component $L\cos\sigma$ is not an independent input but is determined by
the attitude quaternion through the body-to-LVLH
rotation\apxref{sec:aero-force,sec:glide-manifold}, so the slow manifold is
a single scalar constraint on the 13-dimensional translational-rotational
state. The full dynamics carry $J_2$ oblate gravity throughout: the
gravitational acceleration has both a radial component
$g_D(r,\phi)$ and a northward component
$g_N(r,\phi)$\apxref{sec:gravity}, and the general slow-manifold
condition\apxeqref{eq:glide-full} retains both. The simplified scalar
$\Lambda = 1$ is the leading-order reduction under spherical gravity and
small flight-path angle. Retaining $J_2$ is not a refinement but a
necessity, and for a reason sharper than the size of the terms. During
skip maneuvers the northward component $g_N$ produces a heading drift whose
sign is fixed by the hemisphere, so it accumulates across successive skips;
the radial component $g_D$ varies the centrifugal balance at re-entry
between ${+}\,0.16\%$ and ${-}\,0.32\%$ of the leading gravitational term,
changing sign at $35.3^\circ$ latitude, so it accumulates only while the
ground track stays on one side of that parallel and partially cancels
across it. The two effects therefore have different sign structures, no
single worst-case latitude bounds both at once, and the enclosure may be
neither tightened by assuming they reinforce nor loosened by assuming they
cancel\apxref{sec:glide-manifold,rem:j2-skip}.

The loss of tightness is read on the \emph{defect measure} of the rescaled
family. The occupation measures live in the cone
$\Mplus([0,T]\times\calX\times\calU)$ of nonnegative Radon measures,
topologized weakly-$*$ as the dual of $C_0([0,T]\times\calX\times\calU)$.
Writing the weak-$*$ limit as $\mu_\eps\rightharpoonup\mu_0+\nu$, with
$\mu_0$ the limiting glide-manifold measure, the \emph{defect measure}
$\nu\ge 0$ is the nonnegative remainder---the mass that fails to concentrate
on the slow manifold as $\eps_{\mathrm{atm}}\to 0$. On $\nu$ the situation is the measure-theoretic analogue of Lions'
concentration alternative---vanishing, dichotomy, or
concentration~\cite{lions1984concentration1}. The relevant scaling group is
the phugoid time dilation $t\mapsto t/\sqrt{\eps_{\mathrm{atm}}}$ that
localizes each atmospheric pass\apxref{sec:glide-manifold}, so the bubbles
are concentration at a shrinking timescale and it is the dilation half of
the principle---the limit-case modification---that applies, not the
translation (escape-to-infinity) case~\cite{lions1985limit1}.

\subsection{Skeleton, bubbles, residual}
Rather than restore compactness, we extract the profile decomposition the
dichotomy hands us:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a finite-dimensional \emph{skeleton} $S$---the concentration
    component, the glide manifold as a graph over $(r,V)$ in the corridor
    subspace, for which a rigorous characterization with quantitative
    accuracy estimates already exists~\cite{lu2006asymptotic};

  \item one \emph{boundary-layer bubble} per skip event, the analogue of a
    concentrating profile: a compact impulse set $\calI_j$ of dimension
    $\le m$, carried by an internal scaling group at each atmospheric pass,
    with orthogonality read off the scale-and-core separation condition
    that governs concentrating profiles
    generally~\cite{gerard1998description}. The $J_2$ oblate-gravity terms are
    retained in the bubble dynamics: the northward component $g_N$
    introduces a heading drift during each skip, and the
    variation of $g_D$ with latitude modifies the centrifugal balance at
    re-entry. The two carry different sign structures across a multi-skip
    ground track, so both have to be enclosed term by term rather than
    bounded at a single worst-case
    latitude\apxref{sec:glide-manifold,rem:j2-skip};

\begin{openitem}{Wrapping-effect suppression in boundary-layer bubbles}
Boundary-layer bubbles represent discrete atmospheric skip events as
compact impulse sets $\calI_j$ carried by internal scaling groups. Because
our framework fixes time as the independent variable, integrating interval
bounds through highly dynamic skip events will induce severe ``wrapping
effects'' (artificial inflation of bounding boxes). Must develop advanced
intersection geometries (zonotopes, Taylor models, etc.)\ integrated into
the profile decomposition to prove geometries maintain tight corridor
bounds through multiple skip events without the reachable set exploding
prematurely.
\end{openitem}

  \item a \emph{compact residual} with a quantitative bound,
    $O(\sqrt{\eps})$ in the corridor metric, in a norm chosen so that the
    loss of compactness actually disappears rather than merely being
    hidden~\cite{solimini1995note}.
\end{itemize}

The number of bubbles is bounded \emph{a~priori},
$K \le K_{\max}(Q_{\max})$, by the integrated heat-load
budget (a trajectory can only afford so many
skips~\cite{shi1971skip,eggers1958comparative}). That derivation carries a
dependency which has to be discharged rather than assumed: the heat-load
budget is integrated from a stagnation-point correlation whose
chemical-equilibrium assumption is weakest at high altitude, which is
precisely where a skip peaks\apxref{rem:sutton-graves-circularity}. The
wrapping effect to be suppressed
here is not new: for linear systems it is already quantified, and the term
is first order in the time step and exponential in the horizon times the
matrix norm~\cite{leguernic2010reachability}.

\subsection{Almost-orthogonality of the scaling groups}
The multi-parameter separation of the
three scaling groups (atmospheric, attitude, elastic) is the
Bahouri--G\'erard almost-orthogonality argument, transported to defect
measures: two profiles are almost-orthogonal when their scale parameters
diverge, so that their rescaled supports become asymptotically disjoint and
their cross terms vanish in the weak-$*$ pairing on
$\Mplus([0,T]\times\calX\times\calU)$. Establishing this non-interaction for
all three groups simultaneously is the one genuinely new obligation, and it
sits on the critical path.

\begin{openitem}{Bahouri--G\'erard under interval disturbances}
The framework relies on the Bahouri--G\'erard almost-orthogonality argument
to separate the multi-parameter scaling groups (atmospheric, attitude, and
elastic). Under interval aerodynamics, these groups couple with an
adversarial, bounded uncertainty. We must prove the non-interaction theorem
for the defect measures while accounting for interval bounds, guaranteeing
that the gap between the upper and lower aerodynamic bounds does not cause
mass to leak between scaling groups during atmospheric transition,
preserving the compact residual bound $O(\sqrt{\eps})$.
\end{openitem}

The argument worth transporting is the constructive one: splitting a
bounded sequence into an oscillatory and a singular part at each scale
forces those scales to be orthogonal, and iterating extracts a pairwise
orthogonal family, so orthogonality is produced by the construction rather
than assumed by it~\cite{bahouri1999high}.

\subsection{The certified bound}
The target statement is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target}
  \calR_{\mathrm{cert}}(w)
  \;\subseteq\;
  \biggl(
    \underbrace{\Phi_S(\Theta)}_{\text{skeleton}}
    \;\oplus\;
    \underbrace{\bigoplus_{j=1}^{K}\calI_j}_{\text{bubbles}}
  \biggr)
  \;\oplus\;
  \underbrace{\B\!\bigl(C_{\calR}\sqrt{\eps}\bigr)}_{\text{residual}},
\end{equation}
uniformly over the admissible family generated by the uncertainty set, not
along a nominal trajectory. The exact residual corridor constant
$C_{\calR}$ and the maximum bubble count $K_{\max}$ are derived as
explicit, closed-form algebraic expressions directly from the physical
corridor bounds and aerodynamic interval widths, completely independent of
numerical optimization logic. In the spherical-LVLH coordinates the
corridor bounds are box constraints on $(r,V)$, so the interval widths
that enter $C_{\calR}$ and $K_{\max}$ have no wrapping from corridor
intersection---the constants inherit the tightness of the coordinate
choice. Requiring the constants in closed form, rather than as the output
of a solver, is what separates a certificate from a computation: exact
rational certificate representations are known to exist, with a documented
bit-complexity that grows with the number of
variables~\cite{kaltofen2012exact,magron2021exact}.

\begin{openitem}{Analytical derivation of corridor constants}
The ultimate guarantee relies on deriving the residual corridor constant
$C_{\calR}$ and the maximum boundary-layer bubble count
$K_{\max}(Q_{\max})$. The number of bubbles is bounded a priori by the
integrated heat-load budget. We must derive explicit, closed-form algebraic
expressions for $C_{\calR}$ and $K_{\max}$ strictly from the physical
corridor bounds (which are box constraints on $(r,V)$ in the
spherical-LVLH state), the initial conditions, and the aerodynamic
interval widths, entirely without relying on numerical interior-point
solvers.
\end{openitem}

\section{The application: hypersonic entry}\label{sec:application}
%==========================================================================
Entry is not an arbitrary illustration of the machinery above. It supplies
the singular perturbation, the slow manifold and the concentrating events
in one system, and it is a problem on which the exact methods are already
known to fail.

\subsection{Why the exact methods do not reach it}
Exact computation means solving a Hamilton--Jacobi--Isaacs equation on a
grid, which is exponential in state dimension and capped in practice near
four or five states~\parencite{bansal2017hamilton,mitchell2005time}.
A rigid-body entry vehicle carries thirteen states,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:state-rigid}
  x = (r,\lambda,\phi,\, V,\gamma,\psi,\,
       q_0,q_1,q_2,q_3,\, p,q_b,r_b)
  \;\in\;\R^{13},
\end{equation}
and $17+2N_m$ once integrated heat load, wall temperature, ablation
recession, mass loss and $N_m$ aerothermoelastic modes are
coupled~\parencite{mcnamara2011aeroelastic}. The two counts use
``degree of freedom'' in its two customary senses, and it is worth
separating them: the vehicle is six-degree-of-freedom in the
configuration sense (three translational, three rotational), while
\cref{eq:state-rigid} has thirteen coordinates spanning a
twelve-dimensional state manifold once the unit-quaternion constraint
$\norm{\vvec{q}} = 1$ is imposed.

So the exact grid-HJI method quits before the real problem starts. What
makes the measure-theoretic route viable is not that it evades the
dimension but that the bound it produces does not scale with it: the
skeleton is low-dimensional by construction, the bubbles are compact, and
the residual is a single ball.

\subsection{The state space and the corridor}
The translational state is carried in geocentric spherical position
$(r,\lambda,\phi)$ and wind-frame velocity $(V,\gamma,\psi)$ so that the
entry-corridor constraints (bounded altitude, bounded speed, heating and
load-factor limits) are box constraints on states, the natural setting for
interval arithmetic. Attitude is a unit quaternion, free of Euler-angle
singularities. Because interval arithmetic
evaluates transcendentals natively, the traditional algebraic adjunctions
required to force the system into a polynomial
format~\cite{savageau1987recasting} are obsolete. The state space carries only the
physical dimensions---spherical position, wind-frame velocity, attitude
quaternion, body rates, thermal, ablation, and modal states---with no
artificial embeddings.

\begin{openitem}{State-space minimization}
The shift to exact interval arithmetic renders traditional Savageau-style
polynomial adjunctions---the density-square-root embedding
$y = \sqrt{\rho/\rho_0}$ and the polynomial surrogate for wall temperature
(Varma--Savageau--Voit recasting~\cite{savageau1987recasting})---obsolete, as
transcendentals are evaluated natively. These auxiliary states are
eliminated entirely\apxref{sec:thermal}, collapsing the state space to its
minimal physical dimensions and removing a source of wrapping. The
coordinate system is settled: translational position in geocentric
spherical $(r,\lambda,\phi)$, planet-relative velocity in wind-frame
components $(V,\gamma,\psi)$, and attitude as a unit quaternion. This
choice makes the entry corridor a box in state space (tightening interval
intersections) and keeps the equilibrium-glide slow manifold
native\apxref{sec:glide-manifold}. The trade-off---transcendental dynamics versus
polynomial dynamics with nonlinear corridor intersection---is resolved in
favor of the spherical formulation, where wrapping is distributed across
time steps and controlled by step-size refinement rather than concentrated
in corridor intersection\apxref{sec:summary-table}.
\end{openitem}

\section{Plan of work}
%==========================================================================
Six concrete obligations carry the program; they are stated where each
arises above and gathered here, in order, as the plan of work.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \emph{Liouville equation for differential inclusions}: a measure
    embedding valid for the set-valued (interval) dynamics.
  \item \emph{Exact-arithmetic computational pipeline}: a deterministic
    interval integrator that verifies the bounds without floating point.
  \item \emph{Wrapping-effect suppression in boundary-layer bubbles}: tight
    enclosures carried through skip events without premature blow-up.
  \item \emph{Bahouri--G\'erard under interval disturbances}:
    almost-orthogonality of the scaling groups under bounded
    uncertainty---\textbf{the critical path}, on which the profile
    decomposition and the closed-form bound rest.
  \item \emph{Analytical derivation of the corridor constants}: closed-form
    $C_{\calR}$ and $K_{\max}$ from the physical corridor and interval
    widths, free of any numerical solver.
  \item \emph{State-space minimization}: elimination of the Savageau-style
    polynomial adjunctions now that transcendentals are evaluated natively.
\end{enumerate}
Objectives 1--2 establish the interval occupation-measure embedding, 3--4 the
profile decomposition, and 5--6 reduce it to closed form; objective 4 gates
the rest.

\subsection{Sequencing}
The dependency structure suggests three phases rather than six parallel
tracks. The first establishes the embedding---objective~1, with the density
envelope of objective~2 that it needs in order to be stated on a concrete
inclusion\apxref{sec:density}. The second attacks the critical path:
objective~4, which is where the program either works or does not, and which
should therefore be reached early enough that a negative result is still
recoverable. Objective~3 runs alongside it, since the almost-orthogonality
argument and the enclosure geometry constrain each other. The third phase
reduces the decomposition to closed form---objectives~5 and~6---and
completes the exact-arithmetic pipeline, which is the only deliverable that
cannot be shortened by a weaker theorem.

If objective~4 resists a proof for all three scaling groups
simultaneously, the fallback is to establish it for the atmospheric and
attitude groups and to treat the elastic group as a widened interval rather
than a separate profile. That weakens the residual constant without
invalidating the decomposition, and it is worth identifying now as the
retreat position.
