\begin{abstract}
A method for computing \emph{certified} reachable sets of a full 
six-degree-of-freedom hypersonic entry vehicle, reaching state dimensions where
grid-based dynamic programming cannot, with a guarantee that is derived
analytically and verified in exact arithmetic rather than produced by a 
numerical solver.
\end{abstract}

\section*{The problem}
The reachable set of an entry vehicle (every state it can be driven to under admissible control) is what safety and threat analyses need bounded.
Computing it exactly means solving a Hamilton--Jacobi--Isaacs equation on a grid, which is exponential in state dimension and capped in practice near four or five states~\parencite{bansal2017hamilton,mitchell2005time}.
A rigid-body entry vehicle carries thirteen states, $x=(r,\theta,\phi,\,u,v,w,\,q_0\!:\!q_3,\,\omega_{1:3})\in\mathbb{R}^{13}$, and twenty-five once ablation recession, integrated heat load and aerothermoelastic deformation are coupled~\parencite{mcnamara2011aeroelastic}.

\section*{The approach}
Embed the hybrid flight dynamics into a Banach space of occupation measures.
The nonlinear flight mechanics $\dot x=f_q(x,c,t)$ become \emph{linear}:
the Liouville equation is a linear constraint on a measure $\mu$ over $[0,T]\times\mathcal{X}\times U$, and $\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_T$ outer-approximates the reachable set.
So far this is the occupation-measure program of Lasserre, Henrion and collaborators~\parencite{lasserre2008nonlinear,vinter1993convex}.
The embedding buys linearity at the cost of uniqueness, and the way it is recovered is standard: a trajectory family satisfying the Liouville constraint lies in the convex hull of the generalized curves, so measures correspond to convex mixtures of trajectories rather than to one of them~\parencite{henrion2014convex,ball1989version}.
The same relaxation is what admits the interval envelope, and that is the first thing that has to be built rather than borrowed.
Because the true aerodynamics are transcendental, they are enclosed in strict intervals, and a vector field selected from an interval is a set-valued map; the resulting differential inclusion is the object the measure must describe, not the nominal field~\parencite{filippov1988differential,aubin1984differential}.
Occupation-measure theory for inclusions of exactly this kind already exists and must be cited as prior art: it defines an occupational invariant measure without assuming solution uniqueness, which is the same move made here~\parencite{artstein1999invariant}.
What it assumes is worth stating plainly, because it is where the entry problem is likely to fail: the vector fields must satisfy a linear growth condition, and the singular-perturbation results additionally require a uniform bound on the fast state along the whole $\varepsilon$-family~\parencite{artstein1999invariant,artstein2000value}.

\todo[inline]{\textbf{Liouville Equation for Differential Inclusions:}
The current framework embeds the flight mechanics $\dot{x}=f_q(x,c,t)$ as a linear Liouville constraint on an occupation measure $\mu$ over $[0,T]\times\mathcal{X}\times U$. By bounding true non-polynomial aerodynamics within strict intervals, the vector field becomes a differential inclusion $\dot{x} \in \mathcal{F}(x, c, t)$. 
We must formulate a robust Liouville equation capable of acting on set-valued maps, and rigorously prove that the support of the resulting occupation measure, $\mathrm{supp}\,\mu_T$, securely outer-approximates all possible trajectories generated by any vector field selected from the interval inclusion.}
The new content is what happens in the thin-atmosphere limit $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{atm}}=H_s/R_e\to0$.
Rescaled by the phugoid-to-secular time ratio (which, as in the classical Chapman--Loh variables, enters as $\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\mathrm{atm}}}$, not $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{atm}}$~\parencite{chapman1959approximate,loh1968reentry}), the occupation-measure family \emph{is not tight}.
Mass concentrates onto the low-dimensional equilibrium-glide slow manifold $\Lambda=L\cos\sigma/[m(g-V^2/r)]=1$.

This is precisely Lions' concentration alternative, read on the defect measure of the rescaled family~\parencite{lions1984concentration1}.
The bubbles are concentration at a shrinking timescale, so the operative half of the principle is the dilation case rather than the translation case: it is the limit-case modification that applies~\parencite{lions1985limit1}.
Rather than restore compactness, we extract the profile decomposition the dichotomy
hands us:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a finite-dimensional \emph{skeleton} $\mathcal{S}$---the concentration
        component, the glide manifold as a graph over its retained coordinates,
        for which a rigorous characterization with quantitative accuracy
        estimates already exists~\parencite{lu2006asymptotic};
  \item one \emph{boundary-layer bubble} per skip event, the analogue of a
        concentrating profile: a compact impulse set $\mathcal{I}_j$ of dimension
        $\le m$, carried by an internal scaling group at each atmospheric pass,
        with orthogonality read off the scale-and-core separation condition that
        governs concentrating profiles generally~\parencite{gerard1998description};

        Boundary-layer bubbles represent discrete atmospheric skip events as compact impulse sets $\mathcal{I}_j$ carried by internal scaling groups.
        Because our framework fixes time as the independent variable, integrating interval bounds through highly dynamic skip events will induce severe "wrapping effects" (artificial inflation of bounding boxes).
        Must develop advanced intersection geometries (zonotopes, Taylor models, etc.) integrated into the profile decomposition to prove geometries maintain tight corridor bounds through multiple skip events without the reachable set exploding prematurely.}
  \item a \emph{compact residual} with a quantitative bound, $O(\sqrt\varepsilon)$
        in the corridor metric, in a norm chosen so that the loss of compactness
        actually disappears rather than merely being hidden~\parencite{solimini1995note}.

\end{itemize}
The number of bubbles is bounded \emph{a priori}, $K\le K_{\max}(Q_{\max})$, by the
integrated heat-load budget (a trajectory can only afford so many skips~\parencite{shi1971skip,eggers1958comparative}).
The wrapping effect to be suppressed here is not new: for linear systems it is
already quantified, and the term is first order in the time step and exponential
in the horizon times the matrix norm~\parencite{leguernic2010reachability}.
The multi-parameter separation of the three scaling groups (atmospheric, attitude,
elastic) is the Bahouri--G\'erard almost-orthogonality argument, transported to
defect measures; it is the one genuinely new obligation and it sits on the
critical path.

The argument worth transporting is the constructive one: splitting a bounded
sequence into an oscillatory and a singular part at each scale forces those
scales to be orthogonal, and iterating extracts a pairwise orthogonal family,
so orthogonality is produced by the construction rather than assumed by
it~\parencite{bahouri1999high}.
\todo[inline]{\textbf{Bahouri-G\'erard Under Interval Disturbances:} 
The framework relies on the Bahouri-Gérard almost-orthogonality argument to separate the multi-parameter scaling groups (atmospheric, attitude, and elastic). 
Under interval aerodynamics, these groups couple with an adversarial, bounded uncertainty. 
We must prove the non-interaction theorem for the defect measures while accounting for interval bounds, guaranteeing that the gap between the upper and lower aerodynamic bounds does not cause mass to leak between scaling groups during atmospheric transition, preserving the compact residual bound $O(\sqrt{\epsilon})$.
}

The target statement is
\[
\Rcert(w)\ \subseteq\
\Bigl(
\underbrace{\Phi_{\mathcal S}(\Theta)}_{\text{skeleton}}
\ \oplus\ 
\underbrace{\bigoplus_{j=1}^{K}\mathcal{I}_j}_{\text{bubbles}}
\Bigr)
\ \oplus\ 
\underbrace{\mathbb{B}\bigl(C_{\mathcal R}\sqrt\varepsilon\bigr)}_{\text{residual}},
\]

uniformly over the admissible family generated by the uncertainty set, not along a nominal trajectory. 
The exact residual corridor constant $C_{\mathcal R}$ and the maximum bubble count $K_{\max}$ are derived as explicit, closed-form algebraic expressions directly from the physical corridor bounds and aerodynamic interval widths, completely independent of numerical optimization logic.
Requiring the constants in closed form, rather than as the output of a solver, is
what separates a certificate from a computation: exact rational certificate
representations are known to exist, with a documented bit-complexity that grows
with the number of variables~\parencite{kaltofen2012exact,magron2021exact}. 
Because interval arithmetic evaluates transcendentals natively, the traditional algebraic adjunctions required to force the system into a polynomial format~\parencite{savageau1987recasting} are obsolete. The augmented state space is collapsed back to its minimal physical dimensions, retaining only the adjunctions genuinely necessary to avoid coordinate singularities.

\todo[inline]{\textbf{Analytical Derivation of Corridor Constants:} 
The ultimate guarantee relies on deriving the residual corridor constant $C_{\mathcal R}$ and the maximum boundary-layer bubble count $K_{\max}(Q_{\max})$; The number of bubbles is bounded a priori by the integrated heat-load budget.
We must then derive explicit, closed-form algebraic expressions for $C_{\mathcal R}$ and $K_{\max}$ strictly from the physical corridor bounds, initial conditions, and the interval widths of the aerodynamics, entirely without relying on numerical interior-point solvers.}

\todo[inline]{\textbf{State Space Minimization and Kinematic Trade-offs:}
The shift to exact interval arithmetic renders traditional Savageau-style polynomial adjunctions (e.g., $y=\sqrt{\rho/\rho_0}$, $\rho=1/||\vec{r}||$) completely obsolete, as transcendentals are evaluated natively. 
We must formally strip away these algebraic embeddings and collapse the theoretical state space back to its minimal physical dimensions, which inherently tightens bounds by reducing susceptibility to the wrapping effect. 
The remaining open implementation task is determining if retaining purely kinematic adjunctions (such as quaternions to avoid Euler angle singularities) is computationally necessary to prevent interval bounding boxes from artificially inflating, or if the raw 13-state formulation is optimal for the pipeline's intersection geometries.}
